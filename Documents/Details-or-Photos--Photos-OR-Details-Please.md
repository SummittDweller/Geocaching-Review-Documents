Greetings, I am reviewing your new cache and I need to know more about how the cache is actually hidden to make sure it meets the guidelines published at:  http://www.geocaching.com/about/guidelines.aspx.  At a minimum please include the following: - a brief description of the container - material, - color, - size, etc. - a brief description of the location - in a park, - along a trail, - at a residence (with permission of course), - at a business (again, with permission), - in the public ROW, etc. - a more detailed description of the container location - how high off the ground, - inside a hole, - attached to what, - which side of the beacon, - how is it attached... tethered, loosely, tightly, etc. I've recently learned that in some locations new cache listings must include photos of the surrounding area, the container, and its hiding spot.  Iowa has not adopted a requirement like this yet, but feel free to include photos if you have them, it can really help speed up this process. > The geocache is an empty white plastic pill bottle. If you remove the bottle top there is a small DNA tube with a log inside. The empty container is about 4 inches long and 2 inches in diameter. There is a wire attached to the container which hangs loosely on a branch of an oak tree about 5 feet off the ground. The location is across the street from a park. There is a small grove of trees in the public right of way. One can park their car on the east side of the street and enter the grove of trees. The cache is about 50 feet from the street in the 4th tree from the north, on the side of the tree that is nearest the street. You have to walk down an incline and cross a small stream to reach the tree. Note that this indicates the color, material, and size of the container, as well as how it is attached.  It also gives specific details about the "beacon", the oak tree in this case, the location of the beacon, and the location of the container in/on the beacon.  Rough dimensions and directions are also provided. **Please include enough detail so that someone could read your Reviewer Note, or describe your photos, to me so that I could successfully find and retrieve the cache while blindfolded and without a GPS.** Recently, we had a first-time cache owner place a container inside an open, live electrical box.  **Please don't put things in locations that might be dangerous to those who seek them!**  Trying to avoid dangerous situations like that one is a big part of the reason why we ask for PHOTOS of the container in its hiding place, OR sufficient detail to help us determine if the location meets guidelines criteria. Please put all of this information into the Reviewer Note.  **If you have photos to attach be sure to do that in a Reviewer Note before you submit the listing for a review.  For some strange reason the Reviewer Note you must provide when you submit for review will NOT allow you to attach photos.**  Also remember, no reviewer will see your changes until you submit the listing for review, and any conversation we have via Reviewer Notes will be visible only to you and the reviewing team.
